Nasscom–MHFA India Whitepaper on Emotional and Mental Wellbeing in Tech

24 Aug 2026  •  10 min read
Share: Link copied

New report examines how IT companies are addressing employee mental health, based on interviews with 21 industry leaders

India's technology sector is taking visible steps to address employee mental health, according to a new whitepaper released by Nasscom and Mental Health First Aid (MHFA) India, with EY as industry partner. Titled "Emotional and Mental Wellbeing: Current Practices in the Tech Industry – 2026," the report offers one of the most detailed looks yet at how IT and GCC organizations in India are building workplace wellbeing systems.

The whitepaper draws on in-depth interviews with 21 leaders — ranging from CEOs and CTOs to HR heads and clinical psychologists across companies including Wipro, IBM, SAP Labs, Tech Mahindra, Genpact, HCL Healthcare, EY Global Delivery Services, and others. Their insights are organized using the Spectrum Framework, based on WHO Guidelines on Mental Health at Work, which categorizes interventions into three tiers: universal prevention and proactive promotion, targeted early intervention, and intensive individualized care.

Among the report's notable findings, non-clinical wellness frameworks such as mindfulness training and Mental Health First Aid programs have been linked to a 40% reduction in absenteeism at Indian IT firms. The report also highlights how psychological safety, manager training, and peer-support networks such as Wipro's two-decade-old MITR program are becoming central to how organizations approach workplace mental health.

At the same time, the whitepaper doesn't shy away from the gaps. It points out that only 27% of employees currently use workplace mental health resources, and just 9% feel comfortable approaching organizational counsellors underscoring how stigma, confidentiality concerns, and inconsistent program design continue to limit impact. Unlike India's POSH Act for workplace harassment, there is currently no legal mandate requiring companies to offer minimum mental health interventions, leaving most efforts ad hoc.

The report closes with a practical roadmap for organizations covering how to measure employee needs, pilot and socialize new offerings, track impact through KPIs, and build lifecycle- and role-sensitive interventions tailored to different career stages and job types.

For HR leaders, founders, and workplace wellbeing champions, this whitepaper offers a rare, evidence-backed snapshot of what's actually happening inside India's tech companies today — and where the industry still has work to do.
